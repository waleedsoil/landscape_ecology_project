Hypotheses

How the map helps us write and test the hypotheses

Every arrow is a claim with a reasonClick an arrow to read why it is there. When a result surprises us, the arrows on that chain are the first things to question.
A hypothesis is a chainEach one starts at a design factor (aspect or forest type) and ends at something we measure. Pick H1 or H2 to see which arrows it relies on.
Signs multiplyConiferous forest increases canopy closure; canopy closure decreases light at the floor; light increases the daily temperature range. One decrease in the chain, so conifers should have a smaller range (H2).
Disagreeing chains show the riskWhere chains point both ways, the result depends on which process is stronger. That is the case for the O-horizon on the south slope (H1), soil moisture under conifers (H2) and, weakly, pH (H2).
Boxes without a dot are assumptionsWe do not measure decomposition, interception or leaching. The hypotheses assume them, so the poster should say so.
The design removes or records pathsElevation is held constant, so its arrow is faded. Slope angle, cloud cover and texture still act, so we record them as covariates.
Feedback loops act slowlyA thicker O-horizon keeps the soil moister, which speeds decomposition, which thins the O-horizon again. Loops like this shape soils over years, not within our two weeks of logging.
Editing the mapAll boxes, arrows and hypotheses are three lists at the top of assets/js/causal-map.js. Add or change an entry there and the ring, the chains and the panel update on reload.
